Water-Tank PCS and SIS Control System

An independently developed control-system demonstrator combining continuous level control, a modulating inlet valve, VFD-controlled duty/standby pumps, automated pump changeover and an independent three-transmitter protection layer.

Engineering purpose

Project objectives and boundaries

The project demonstrates how normal process control, equipment protection and an independent shutdown layer can be separated while still coordinating their responses to abnormal conditions.

Control objectives

  • Maintain tank level close to the operator-selected setpoint.
  • Coordinate inlet-valve position and discharge-pump speed.
  • Operate two VFD pumps in duty/standby configuration.
  • Perform automated, bumpless pump changeover.
  • Detect pump faults, transmitter faults and abnormal levels.
  • Prevent pump dry running and uncontrolled tank filling.

Project boundaries

  • The process and instrument values are simulated.
  • Setpoints are illustrative rather than plant-design values.
  • No employer drawings, logic or operating data are used.
  • Final-element sizing and SIL verification are excluded.
  • The ESP32 is used only as an educational logic demonstrator.
  • The project must not be applied directly to an operating plant.

Control philosophy

Normal level-control strategy

Split-range control

LT-101 provides the normal process level to the PCS level controller. The controller produces a common response index used to coordinate inlet flow and outlet pumping.

  • At low level, the inlet valve opens further and pump speed reduces toward its minimum stable operating speed.
  • Near setpoint, the inlet valve and duty pump operate around configured bias values.
  • At high level, the inlet valve closes progressively and the duty-pump speed increases.
  • Output tracking and configured ramp rates minimise process disturbances during mode changes.

Transmitter failure direction

The PCS level signal is configured with upscale burnout handling. A detected bad-quality or out-of-range signal raises an alarm and prevents uncontrolled automatic response.

  • Automatic pump starting is blocked.
  • Controller outputs move to a configured safe bias.
  • The operator receives a transmitter-fault alarm.
  • Protection remains available through the separate voting transmitters.
Split-range limits, overlap, deadband and bias values must be established from pump curves, valve characteristics and dynamic process testing. They are not universal constants.
Equipment sequencing

Duty/standby pump management

Pump availability

A pump is considered available only when all required permissives are healthy.

  • Local emergency stop healthy.
  • VFD ready and communication healthy.
  • No motor overload or active VFD fault.
  • Remote operating mode selected.
  • Minimum suction level satisfied.
  • No active maintenance inhibit.
  • Protection trip reset.

Bumpless changeover sequence

  1. Confirm the standby pump is available.
  2. Issue the standby-pump start command.
  3. Confirm run feedback before the start timeout expires.
  4. Ramp the incoming pump to the tracked operating-speed demand.
  5. Ramp the outgoing pump toward its minimum operating speed.
  6. Stop the outgoing pump and confirm stopped feedback.
  7. Transfer the duty designation to the running replacement.

Detected pump faults

Fault Detection Control response
Start failure Run feedback absent after the configured start timeout. Cancel start, mark pump unavailable and start the healthy standby pump.
Unexpected stop Run feedback is lost while a run command remains active. Alarm, remove the failed pump command and initiate standby changeover.
VFD or motor fault VFD fault, motor overload or protection relay input. Stop the affected pump, latch the fault and transfer duty when the standby unit is available.
Speed deviation Actual speed differs from demand beyond tolerance for a configured delay. Raise an alarm and initiate changeover if pumping performance is inadequate.
No-flow condition Pump running with low discharge flow after a proving delay. Stop the pump to prevent damaging operation and attempt standby transfer.
Both pumps unavailable Neither pump satisfies its availability permissives. Raise a high-priority alarm and limit or close inlet flow as tank level rises.
Independent protection

Voting, trips and reset philosophy

LT-201A, LT-201B and LT-201C provide independent measurements to the protection demonstrator. Two agreeing channels are required to initiate a level trip.

Initiating event Detection Automatic action Reset condition
High-high level 2oo3 transmitters above LAHH Close and inhibit opening of the inlet valve; raise a latched high-high alarm. Keep an eligible discharge pump available unless a separate downstream hazard requires it to stop. Level below the reset threshold, initiating condition healthy and manual reset requested.
Low-low level 2oo3 transmitters below LALL Trip and inhibit both discharge pumps to prevent dry running; raise a latched low-low alarm. Level above the reset threshold, pump permissives healthy and manual reset requested.
Transmitter disagreement Channel deviation exceeds the configured tolerance and delay. Raise a protection-system degraded alarm and identify the disagreeing channel. Measurements agree within tolerance and the fault is acknowledged.
Invalid protection channel Bad quality, out-of-range input or diagnostic failure. Annunciate degraded operation and inhibit automatic trip reset. Channel health restored and voting state verified.
A real SIS requires an approved Safety Requirements Specification, hazard analysis, independence review, SIL verification, certified hardware, proof-test procedures and controlled management of bypasses and overrides.

Illustrative level setpoints

Parameter Simulation value Purpose

Control-system implementation

Conceptual I/O allocation

Signal group Typical signals Purpose
PCS analogue inputs Tank level, pump speed feedback, discharge flow and pressure. Process control, validation and equipment monitoring.
PCS analogue outputs Inlet-valve demand and Pump A/B speed references. Modulating control of inlet and outlet capacity.
PCS digital inputs VFD ready, run feedback, fault, overload, remote mode and emergency-stop health. Permissives, equipment status and fault detection.
PCS digital outputs Pump start/stop commands and alarm indications. Equipment sequencing and operator notification.
Protection analogue inputs LT-201A, LT-201B and LT-201C. Independent 2oo3 level voting.
Protection outputs Inlet trip override, pump low-low trip and PCS trip-status indication. Protective action and status communication.
